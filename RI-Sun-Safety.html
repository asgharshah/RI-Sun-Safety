Sun Exposure Context · Rhode Island
County-level sun exposure context
Sun exposure and melanoma risk vary by geography, outdoor activity patterns, and access to shade. The summaries below are qualitative public health guidance intended to complement, not replace, official cancer surveillance data from NCI State Cancer Profiles.

Skin cancer in skin of color

Melanoma can occur in all skin tones. In patients with darker skin phototypes, melanoma is more likely to be diagnosed at a later stage and may present in less commonly examined sites — palms, soles, nails, and mucosal surfaces. Any new, changing, bleeding, or nonhealing lesion warrants evaluation regardless of skin tone. Dermoscopy has demonstrated utility in differentiating benign from malignant lesions across phototypes and can improve early detection in populations historically underserved by dermatology care.

Prevention
Know your ABCDEs
When examining your skin, look for these warning signs. See a dermatologist or your primary care provider if any lesion changes or concerns you.
A
Asymmetry
One half does not match the other
B
Border
Irregular, ragged, or blurred edges
C
Color
Multiple shades of brown, black, red, or white
D
Diameter
Larger than a pencil eraser (~6mm)
E
Evolving
Any change in size, shape, color, or new symptom
